# Trading and Exchanges: Market Microstructure for Practitioners

Larry Harris explains orders, order-driven markets, brokers, liquidity, bid/ask spreads, transaction-cost measurement, and performance evaluation. Oxford University Press lists the book as published in 2002 and provides the chapter outline and abstract. [Publisher record](https://academic.oup.com/book/52292)

**Why it matters for PulseQuant:** fees alone do not describe a fill. The project proposes bid/ask simulation and live order-book analysis, so spread, queue priority, liquidity, and execution assumptions can change whether a predicted edge survives.

**Apply it to:** specify the order types and fill rules in paper trading; distinguish quoted spread from realized slippage; test thin and fast markets; include execution costs in every strategy comparison.

It is not India-specific, so use current NSE and broker rules for local mechanics. It explains market behavior; it does not show that a forecast has alpha.
